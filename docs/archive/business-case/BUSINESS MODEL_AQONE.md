# **Executive Summary**

AqOne is a maritime safety technology venture designed to help municipal and small-scale fishers communicate and access emergency support beyond cellular coverage. The solution combines solar-powered LoRa buoys, a smartphone application, and an operations dashboard supported by weather, distress, and drift analysis.The proposed business model is institutional. Local government units and public-safety or fisheries institutions are the intended paying customers, while municipal fishers are the principal end users. The model avoids requiring each fisher to purchase the core buoy infrastructure.

The immediate business objective is to validate AqOne through the New Washington, Aklan pilot, establish actual deployment and operating costs, secure institutional partners, and develop a repeatable deployment model that can scale to other coastal municipalities.

# **Business Model**

| Business Model Element | AqOne |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Primary Customer / Buyer** | LGUs, MDRRMO, BFAR, PCG, and other institutional/public-safety partners. |
| **End Users** | Municipal and small-scale fishers. |
| **Core Value** | Shared maritime safety infrastructure that extends communication beyond cellular coverage. |
| **Delivery Model** | Institutional deployment, onboarding, training, maintenance, and technical support. |

| **Recurring Revenue** | Annual software, monitoring, maintenance, and technical support. |

AqOne should be positioned as a managed maritime-safety infrastructure service, not simply as a mobile application.

# **Unit Economics**

| Metric | Definition |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Deployment Gross Margin** | Deployment revenue less direct hardware, installation, commissioning, and other direct deployment costs. |
| **Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR)** | Annual contracted support and maintenance revenue from active institutional customers. |
| **Recurring Cost per Municipality** | Cloud, monitoring, maintenance, support, and other recurring costs attributable to one active deployment. |
| **Customer Acquisition Cost** | Sales, partnership, demonstration, and procurement effort required to secure one institutional customer. |
| **Payback Period** | Time required for gross contribution from a customer to recover acquisition and onboarding costs. |
| **Renewal Rate** | Percentage of institutional customers renewing annual service agreements. |

 

# **Scaling Strategy**

| Stage | Focus | Scale Objective |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Stage 1 — Pilot** | New Washington, Aklan | Validate technology, adoption, safety outcomes, and true cost per deployment. |
| **Stage 2 — Local Replication** | Additional coastal municipalities in Aklan | Standardize deployment, training, procurement, and maintenance. |
| **Stage 3 — Provincial Scale** | Aklan-wide | Build centralized support and recurring service operations. |
| **Stage 4 — Regional Expansion** | Other Philippine coastal municipalities | Replicate the standardized deployment model through institutional partnerships. |
| **Stage 5 — National Scale** | Broader Philippine coastal market | Develop repeatable government/institutional procurement and managed-service operations. |

 

Scaling priorities:

·        Standardize buoy hardware and firmware.

·        Standardize installation, testing, maintenance, and training.

·        Use one software platform across municipalities.

·        Track cost per municipality and cost per coverage unit.

·        Build recurring service revenue rather than relying only on new deployments.

·        Use pilot results and institutional references to shorten future procurement cycles.
